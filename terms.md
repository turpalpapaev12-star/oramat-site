## What Oramat is

Oramat is a free app that helps you remember to water your plants. You tell it when you watered something, and it estimates how dry the soil is likely to be now. It can optionally read from a soil moisture sensor.

It is made by one person, in Belgium, as a personal project. It is not a business, not a professional service, and it comes with no guarantees.

## Plant care information is general guidance, not expert advice

The care information in the app — light, placement, watering style, thresholds, tips — comes from general horticultural guidance. It is a **starting point**, not a diagnosis.

Real plants vary enormously with soil, pot size, room temperature, humidity, season and light. The app cannot see your plant. If the app says one thing and your plant says another, **believe your plant.**

Oramat cannot diagnose plant disease, pests, or nutrient problems. For a plant you care about, ask a nursery or a horticulturist.

## ⚠️ Pet and child safety — read this

The app marks plants as toxic or non-toxic to cats and dogs based on the ASPCA Animal Poison Control plant database. **Treat this as a rough first warning, not a safety guarantee.**

Specifically, be aware that:

- **A plant marked "safe" may still be harmful.** Toxicity varies between varieties, and eating any plant can make an animal ill. "Non-toxic" in the ASPCA sense means "not known to poison," not "harmless."
- **Species are grouped.** The app lists broad types, like "Cactus" or "Succulent," which cover many species with different properties. Your specific plant may not match the entry you selected.
- **Identification is yours, not ours.** You choose the plant type from a list. If you pick the wrong one, every warning in the app becomes wrong with it.
- **Toxicity varies by part of the plant.** Leaves, sap, seeds and fruit can differ. A tomato's fruit is fine for people; its leaves are toxic to pets.
- **Children are not covered at all.** The data is about cats and dogs. Do not use it to judge whether a plant is safe around a child.

**If an animal or a person has eaten part of a plant, do not consult this app. Contact a vet or a doctor immediately.** Show them the botanical name, which the app displays for exactly this reason.

If you have pets or small children, verify any plant yourself before bringing it home. The ASPCA database is free and public: https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/toxic-and-non-toxic-plants

## Moisture readings and estimates

**Without a sensor**, the moisture figure is an *estimate* calculated from when you last said you watered and a typical drying interval for that plant type. It is a guess. It does not know your soil, your pot, or your radiator.

**With a sensor**, readings depend on your hardware, your calibration and where the probe sits in the pot. Sensors drift, corrode, lose signal and run out of battery. A reading can be stale or simply wrong.

Do not rely on Oramat alone for a plant that matters to you. Check the soil with your finger.
